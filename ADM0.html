ADM Phase: Preliminary
Core Purpose & Scope

The Preliminary Phase prepares the enterprise to undertake successful EA initiatives [cite: 1, 4]. It defines where, what, why, who, and how architecture is conducted in the organization [cite: 1]. It establishes the Architecture Capability (roles, processes, budget, tools) [cite: 1, 4], scopes affected enterprise units [cite: 1, 4], defines Architecture Principles [cite: 1, 2, 4], and tailors the TOGAF 10 Framework [cite: 1, 3, 4].

Objective 1: Determine Capability Desired
  • Review organizational context, business goals, drivers, and culture [cite: 3, 4].
  • Identify & scope affected enterprise elements (Core vs. Soft units) [cite: 1, 4].
  • Identify intersecting frameworks (e.g., COBIT, ITIL, PMBOK) [cite: 3, 4].
  • Establish maturity target using Series Guide: Architecture Maturity Models [cite: 3, 4].
Objective 2: Establish Capability
  • Define & establish the organizational model for Enterprise Architecture [cite: 4].
  • Define detailed governance processes, structures, and Architecture Board [cite: 1, 4].
  • Select and implement supporting EA tools & repository structures [cite: 1, 4].
  • Define enduring Architecture Principles [cite: 1, 2, 4].
Candidate 3-Bullet Elevator Pitch (Exam Anchor)
  • Where, What, Why, Who, How: The Preliminary Phase defines where architecture applies, who performs it, why it is needed, and how it is governed [cite: 1].
  • Just Enough Capability: Build EA capability only to the maturity level required for current work; expand iteratively as needed [cite: 1, 4].
  • Tailored Foundation: Customise TOGAF terminology, processes, and content before initiating Phase A [cite: 1, 3, 4].
Condensed 3-Bullet Step Summary
  • Scope & Boundaries: Identify Core enterprise units (direct EA value) and Soft units (indirect capability change) [cite: 1].
  • Governance & Organization: Allocate roles, set up the Architecture Board, and establish governance processes [cite: 1, 4].
  • Tailoring & Tooling: Tailor TOGAF (terminology, processes, content) and implement EA repository tools [cite: 1, 4].
Step Acronym Mnemonic: S-C-D-I-T-D ("Smarter Enterprise EA Teams Execute Architecture Integration")

Identify Core Enterprise Units (units receiving direct architecture attention and achieving most value) and Soft Enterprise Units (units seeing change to their capability, but not directly affected) [cite: 1].

Identify impacted stakeholders and governance bodies [cite: 1].

Understand how architectural material (standards, guidelines, compliance reports) is brought under governance [cite: 1].

Determine ownership for artifacts and establish governance for implementation and change [cite: 1].

Assess budget requirements, allocate key roles & responsibilities, create EA processes, and form architecture teams [cite: 1, 4].

Develop enduring rules and guidelines across Business, Data, Application, and Technology domains in close cooperation with key stakeholders [cite: 1, 2].

Adapt three specific topics: Terminology, Processes, and Content [cite: 1]. Integrate TOGAF with existing management frameworks [cite: 3, 4].

Implement tools (ranging from simple file repositories to professional EA management software) and techniques (decision-making, workshops, modeling) aligned to target maturity [cite: 1].

High-Yield Fact: Enterprise Boundary

An enterprise is the highest level of description used to set the boundary for EA work. It can be a whole corporation, a business unit (e.g., R&D), or a coalition of multiple organizations [cite: 3].

ADM Technique: Principles
Principles Definition & Purpose

Architecture principles represent general rules and guidelines for the use of resources and assets across the enterprise [cite: 2]. They provide the foundation for making future architecture decisions, reflecting consensus among stakeholders [cite: 2]. They are intended to be enduring and seldom amended [cite: 2].

Enterprise vs Architecture Principles

Enterprise Principles: Provide a basis for decision-making throughout the enterprise and support overall mission [cite: 2].

Architecture Principles: Govern architecture work (development, maintenance, and use of EA across BDAT domains) [cite: 2].

Standard 4-Part Structure
  • Name: Easy to remember identifier [cite: 2].
  • Statement: Short, crisp fundamental rule [cite: 2].
  • Rationale: Business benefit of adherence [cite: 2].
  • Implications: Specific impact on resources, costs, activities [cite: 2].
5 Criteria of a Good Principle (HIGH YIELD EXAM TOPIC)
C - C - S - U - R

("Can Computer Science Understand Reasoning?") [cite: 2]

1. Complete: Each principle is defined and every perceived situation is covered [cite: 2].
2. Consistent: Expressed without contradictions to allow balanced interpretations [cite: 2].
3. Stable: Enduring over time, yet accommodates environmental changes [cite: 2].
4. Understandable: Clear, unambiguous intentions that minimize violations [cite: 2].
5. Robust: Enables consistent quality decisions even in complex situations [cite: 2].
Standard Principle Example: Compliance with Law

Name: Compliance with Law [cite: 2]

Statement: Information management processes comply with all relevant laws and policies [cite: 2].

Rationale: Abiding by legal mandates prevents penalties and reputational damage [cite: 2].

Implications: Enterprise must implement data retention controls, audit access, and track legal changes [cite: 2].

Principle Lifecycle in Trade-Off Governance

When a project team proposed storing customer financial data in an unencrypted public cloud storage bucket to meet a tight release deadline, the Architecture Board cited the principle Compliance with Law and Data Security to override the project team, requiring mandatory encryption at rest [cite: 2].

ADM Lifecycle of Principles

Preliminary Phase: Identified and established [cite: 2].

Phase A: Reviewed and confirmed [cite: 2].

Phases B, C, D: Applied and fulfilled during architecture development [cite: 2].

Principles Exam Quiz

Which criteria ensures that an architecture principle is expressed without contradictions?

Correct Answer: B (Consistent)
Explanation: Consistency ensures the principle is expressed without internal contradictions [cite: 2].

Framework Customization
Framework Tailoring Basics

TOGAF must be adapted to organizational needs [cite: 1, 3, 4]. Tailoring adapts three main topics: Terminology, Processes, and Content [cite: 1].

Maturity Assessment & Scaling Rule

Regularly evaluate current EA capability with an enterprise EA maturity assessment [cite: 3, 4]. Important rule: Only build up EA capability to the level of maturity needed for current architecture work [cite: 4].

Series Guides for Capability
  • Architecture Maturity Models — Techniques for evaluating EA maturity [cite: 4].
  • TOGAF Leader's Guide to Establishing and Evolving an EA Capability — Practices for EA management [cite: 4].
Case Study: Tailoring TOGAF for Agile Teams

A fintech enterprise integrated TOGAF ADM with Scrum/SAFe frameworks [cite: 3], creating two-week architecture sprints for Phase B/C modeling to align with product development cycles.

Exam Rule: Do not aim for maximum maturity (Level 5) automatically. Build only what is needed for current work [cite: 4].

Governance & Organization
Governance Setup Purpose

Establish governance and support frameworks, define ownership for artifacts, assign key EA roles, and setup an Enterprise Architecture Board [cite: 1, 4].

Governance Questions to Answer
  • Who has ownership for which architectural artifact? [cite: 1]
  • How do we govern implementation and change? [cite: 1]
  • How does EA interact with project management and ITIL? [cite: 3, 4]
Key Artifact: Organizational Model for EA

Includes EA budget requirements, key role allocations, architecture process definitions, and Architecture Board charters [cite: 1, 4].

Architecture Board Setup in Healthcare

A healthcare enterprise established an Architecture Board meeting bi-weekly to resolve architectural compliance waivers and maintain repository integrity [cite: 1, 2, 4].

High-Yield Fact: Governance structure must be established or modified in the Preliminary Phase to support EA management [cite: 1, 4].
