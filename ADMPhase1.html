Preliminary Phase: Architecture Capability

Core Value Proposition

Establishes "Where, What, Why, Who & How" EA operates.

Primary Purpose

The Preliminary Phase creates the Architecture Capability for the enterprise. It prepares the organization to undertake successful Enterprise Architecture projects by customizing TOGAF and defining governance rules.

Executed prior to initiating full ADM cycles.

The 5 Core Architectural Questions

Where

Define the Enterprise Scope & organizational boundaries.

Why

Identify key drivers, business imperatives & context.

What

Define requirements & desired architectural scope.

Who

Establish the EA Team, roles, & Organizational Model.

How

Formulate Architecture Principles, Governance, & Repository tooling.

Preliminary Phase Activity Workflow

Select a step below to inspect detailed activity breakdowns, iterations, and agile integrations.

Step 1: Scope the Enterprise Organizations Impacted

Identify core business units, departments, strategic alliances, and external partner ecosystems that will fall under the authority and governance of the EA capability.

  • Determine enterprise breadth (core enterprise vs business unit scope).
  • Assess organizational authority limits and stakeholder alignment.
  • Establish impact on existing strategic planning cycles.
Step 2: Confirm Governance and Support Frameworks

Review and align intersecting management frameworks operating in the enterprise (e.g., ITIL, COBIT, PRINCE2, SAFe) to ensure unified governance.

  • Map EA touchpoints into project management and software delivery cycles.
  • Align EA compliance reviews with corporate risk governance.
Step 3: Define and Establish EA Team and Organization

Formalize the EA team structure, define architect roles, skill requirements (e.g., TOGAF enterprise architect, domain architects), and establish the Architecture Board.

  • Establish reporting lines to C-level executives.
  • Define EA team capability maturity targets.
Step 4: Identify and Establish Architecture Principles

Formulate enduring rules and guidelines across Business, Data, Application, and Technology domains that govern all future target architectures.

  • Ensure principles meet criteria: Complete, Consistent, Stable, Understandable, Robust.
  • Use the standard 4-part structure: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications.
Step 5: Tailor the TOGAF Framework

Adapt TOGAF Fundamental Content, ADM phases, terminology, and deliverables to match the enterprise's unique terminology and culture.

  • Incorporate relevant TOGAF 10 Series Guides (e.g., Agile, Digital, Security).
  • Define deliverable templates and metamodel extensions.
Step 6: Develop Tooling Strategy & Implementation Plan

Select and implement EA repository tools, modeling software (e.g., ArchiMate integration), and repository classification structures.

  • Configure the Architecture Repository (Landscape, Standards, Requirements).
  • Generate the formal **Request for Architecture Work** to trigger Phase A.

Phase Inputs

  • Board Strategies & Business Plans: High-level business context, strategic drivers, and corporate goals.
  • Intersecting Management Frameworks: Existing IT, project governance, COBIT, ITIL, or SAFe standards.
  • Existing Enterprise Descriptions: Legacy IT documentation, business models, and system diagrams.

Deliverables & Outputs

  • Tailored Architecture Framework: Customized ADM, methods, and deliverable structures.
  • Organizational Model for EA: Team structure, roles, responsibilities, and Architecture Board charter.
  • Architecture Principles Catalog: Enduring rules for Business, Data, App, & Technology.
  • Request for Architecture Work: Sponsoring document triggering ADM Phase A cycle.

Case Study: Global Retail Bank Modernization

Executing the Preliminary Phase for a Tier-1 Financial Institution

Problem

The bank faced fragmented IT systems across 12 countries, uncoordinated cloud adoption, high legacy maintenance costs, and frequent regulatory compliance failures due to lack of central EA governance.

EA Application

Executed Preliminary Phase: Scoped global divisions, tailored TOGAF 10 with ITIL & SAFe, established an Architecture Board chaired by the CTO, and defined 8 core Architecture Principles.

Results & Outputs

Issued approved **Request for Architecture Work**, configured a central EA Repository, reduced duplicate software spend by 25%, and established automated compliance gates.

Execution Highlights in the Bank's Preliminary Phase

1. EA Capability Maturity Target:

Moved from Level 1 (Ad Hoc) to Level 3 (Defined Capability) over 12 months using TOGAF capability targets.

2. Tooling & Repository Setup:

Deployed an ArchiMate-compliant modeling tool connected to Jira and ServiceNow for real-time application portfolio tracking.

Exam Traps & High-Yield Rules

  • Trap 1: Request for Architecture Work The Request for Architecture Work is an output of the Preliminary Phase and an input to Phase A. It is NOT generated in Phase A.
  • Trap 2: Preliminary Phase Scope The Preliminary Phase is about establishing the capability to do architecture, NOT creating the architecture vision or target models itself.
  • Mnemonic: NSRI (Principles Structure) All TOGAF principles must have: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications.

TOGAF 10th Edition Key Updates

Modular Structure:

TOGAF 10 separates content into TOGAF Fundamental Content (core concepts & ADM) and TOGAF Series Guides (Agile, Digital, Security).

Agile & Digital Integration:

Preliminary Phase now explicitly guides tailoring the ADM for concurrent execution alongside Agile development frameworks (e.g., SAFe, Scrum).
