Preliminary Phase: Architecture Capability

Core Value Proposition

Establishes the "Where, What, Why, Who, and How" of Enterprise Architecture capability[cite: 2].

💡
Must-Know Foundation Fact: The Preliminary Phase is the foundational preparation phase executed before initiating formal ADM cycles (Phases A through H)[cite: 1, 2]. Its main goal is to create and enable the enterprise's Architecture Capability, customize the TOGAF framework, and establish governing Architecture Principles[cite: 1, 2].

Primary Purpose

Prepare the enterprise for successful architecture work[cite: 2]. This includes tailoring the TOGAF framework[cite: 1, 2], defining the Organizational Model for Enterprise Architecture[cite: 1, 2], establishing governance resources[cite: 1, 2], and formulating enduring Architecture Principles[cite: 1, 2].

Precedes all cyclic ADM phase iterations[cite: 1].

The 5 Core Architectural Questions (Approach)[cite: 2]

1. Where

Define the enterprise scope & impacted organizational boundaries[cite: 1, 2].

2. Why

Identify key drivers, business imperatives & strategic context[cite: 1, 2].

3. What

Set requirements & desired architecture capability scope[cite: 1, 2].

4. Who

Establish the EA Team, roles, & Organizational Model[cite: 1, 2].

5. How

Formulate Architecture Principles, Governance framework, & tooling strategy[cite: 1, 2].

Core Purpose & Fundamental Content Alignment

The Preliminary Phase creates an operational framework that allows Enterprise Architecture teams to operate seamlessly alongside other governance capabilities[cite: 1, 2]. By establishing clear reporting lines, C-level executive support, and standard tooling, the phase ensures that future architecture iterations deliver measurable business value[cite: 1, 2].

Branch 1: The 5 Core Architectural Questions

The Preliminary Phase approach establishes the architecture capability by addressing 5 fundamental questions[cite: 2]:

1. WHERE (Scope) Defines enterprise scope and impacted organizational boundaries[cite: 1, 2].
2. WHY (Context) Identifies strategic business goals, drivers, and context[cite: 1, 2].
3. WHAT (Requirements) Sets the specific requirements for the architecture capability[cite: 1, 2].
4. WHO (Team) Establishes the EA team, roles, skills, and Architecture Board[cite: 1, 2].
5. HOW (Governance & Tools) Formulates Architecture Principles, governance frameworks, and repository tools[cite: 1, 2].

Branch 2: The 6 Setup Steps Workflow

Execution of the Preliminary Phase is organized into 6 systematic steps[cite: 1, 2]:

Step 1: Scope Enterprise Define affected organizations and boundaries[cite: 1, 2].
Step 2: Confirm Frameworks Align ITIL, COBIT, SAFe, & project management[cite: 1, 2].
Step 3: Establish EA Team Form Organizational Model for EA & Board charter[cite: 1, 2].
Step 4: Define Principles Formulate enduring BDAT architecture principles[cite: 1, 2].
Step 5: Tailor TOGAF Customize ADM content & include Series Guides[cite: 1, 2].
Step 6: Deploy Tools Strategy Setup Architecture Repository & issue Request for Work[cite: 1, 2].

Exam Pitfall: Do not confuse Architecture Principles (defined in the Preliminary Phase)[cite: 1, 2] with Business Principles (which are confirmed and elaborated in Phase A)[cite: 1]. Preliminary Phase Step-by-Step Activities[cite: 1, 2]

Select a step below to inspect detailed activity breakdowns, inputs, and high-yield study notes.

Step 1: Scope the Enterprise Organizations Impacted[cite: 1, 2]

Identify core business units, departments, strategic partners, and external ecosystems affected by the Architecture Capability[cite: 1, 2]. Enterprise scope can range from the entire corporation down to a single business unit or division[cite: 1].

  • Determine organizational breadth and authority boundaries[cite: 1, 2].
  • Identify impacted business units and executive sponsors[cite: 1, 2].
  • Assess existing strategic planning and budgeting cycles[cite: 1, 2].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The scope of the architecture activity is bounded by financial resources, organizational authority, and stakeholder concerns[cite: 1].

Step 2: Confirm Governance and Support Frameworks[cite: 1, 2]

Review and align intersecting management frameworks operating in the enterprise, such as ITIL for service management, COBIT for IT governance, SAFe/Scrum for agile software development, and PRINCE2/PMBOK for project management[cite: 1, 2].

  • Map EA touchpoints into project delivery and agile sprint backlogs[cite: 1, 2].
  • Align architecture compliance checkpoints with corporate risk governance[cite: 1, 2].
  • Establish touchpoints with business portfolio management[cite: 1, 2].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: TOGAF is designed to complement and integrate seamlessly with non-TOGAF management frameworks[cite: 1].

Step 3: Define and Establish EA Team and Organization[cite: 1, 2]

Formalize the EA team structure, define architect roles and skill profiles, establish executive reporting lines, and form the Architecture Board to oversee architectural decisions[cite: 1, 2].

  • Define roles and responsibilities for Enterprise Architects and Domain Architects[cite: 1, 2].
  • Establish the Architecture Board charter, decision rights, and meeting cadences[cite: 1, 2].
  • Assess skill gaps and establish training plans for the EA team[cite: 1, 2].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The Organizational Model for EA formally defines team structure, decision rights, and governance roles[cite: 1, 2].

Step 4: Identify and Establish Architecture Principles[cite: 1, 2]

Develop enduring general rules and guidelines across Business, Data, Application, and Technology domains that govern all future target architectures and implementation decisions[cite: 1, 2].

  • Formulate principles using the standard 4-part structure: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications[cite: 1].
  • Validate principles against the 5 key criteria: Complete, Consistent, Stable, Understandable, Robust[cite: 1].
  • Secure formal endorsement from executive leadership and the Architecture Board[cite: 1].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: Architecture Principles are enduring rules intended to guide decisions and are seldom amended once ratified[cite: 1].

Step 5: Tailor the TOGAF Framework[cite: 1, 2]

Adapt TOGAF Fundamental Content, ADM phases, deliverables, and terminology to match the enterprise's unique culture, operating model, and technical domain requirements[cite: 1, 2].

  • Integrate relevant TOGAF Series Guides (e.g., Security Architecture, Agile Integration, Digital Enterprise)[cite: 1].
  • Customize deliverable templates and define enterprise metamodel extensions[cite: 1, 2].
  • Document the Tailored Architecture Framework[cite: 1, 2].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: Tailoring produces the approved deliverable known as the Tailored Architecture Framework[cite: 1, 2].

Step 6: Develop Strategy & Implementation Plan for Tools[cite: 1, 2]

Select and deploy EA modeling software, configure the Architecture Repository classification structure, and generate the formal Request for Architecture Work to initiate ADM Phase A[cite: 1, 2].

  • Configure repository partitions for Landscape, Standards Library, and Governance Repository[cite: 1].
  • Implement modeling tools aligned with ArchiMate or UML standards[cite: 1].
  • Issue the Request for Architecture Work to trigger the first formal ADM cycle[cite: 1, 2].

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The primary triggering output sent from the Preliminary Phase to Phase A is the Request for Architecture Work[cite: 1, 2].

Comprehensive Deep-Dive Scenario

Apex Global Financial Services: Preliminary Phase Implementation

Enterprise: 14 Countries | 45,000 Employees | $12B Revenue

This scenario illustrates how a multinational financial organization established its Enterprise Architecture capability from scratch in response to fragmented technology investments, regulatory friction, and digital channel disruption.

1. Baseline Problem Statement & Strategic Drivers

Fragmented IT & Silos

Each of the 14 regional operating units maintained independent IT teams, resulting in 18 distinct core banking engines and 45% duplicate software licensing costs.

Regulatory & Compliance Friction

Lack of unified data governance caused multi-million dollar regulatory fines under GDPR and EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) rules.

Slow Digital Velocity

New mobile banking features took 14 months to launch due to uncoordinated vendor contracts, lack of API standards, and absence of an architecture review board.

3. Detailed Step-by-Step Execution Workflow at Apex Financial

Click each step below to see how the EA Lead executed the Preliminary Phase steps in practice[cite: 1, 2].

Step 1 Action: Scoping the Enterprise Organization

The Lead Architect conducted executive interviews with the CEO, CTO, and Regional Business Heads[cite: 1, 2]. They established that the Enterprise Architecture Capability would cover all 3 business divisions (Retail, Wealth, Corporate) across all 14 country entities, while excluding non-controlled joint ventures[cite: 1, 2].

  • Boundary: 14 country entities, 45,000 workforce, $12B revenue scope.
  • Authority: Enterprise Architecture Board given veto power over technology spend over $250k.
Step 2 Action: Confirming Governance & Intersecting Frameworks

Apex mapped touchpoints between the tailored TOGAF ADM and existing operating frameworks[cite: 1, 2]:

  • SAFe Agile Framework: EA team sets architecture epics and guardrails; agile squads execute sprint deliverables within defined architecture constraints.
  • ITIL v4: Service transition checkpoints aligned with EA compliance reviews.
  • COBIT 2019: Enterprise risk and compliance metrics mapped directly to Architecture Board reviews.
Step 3 Action: Establishing the EA Team & Organizational Model

Formed a hub-and-spoke EA team structure[cite: 1, 2]. A central Corporate EA team (6 architects) defines global standards, while 8 Domain Architects embedded in regional business units ensure localized alignment[cite: 1, 2]. Established the global Architecture Board with bi-weekly meeting cadence[cite: 1, 2].

Step 4 Action: Formulating Enduring Architecture Principles

Drafted 6 core principles using the standard Name, Statement, Rationale, and Implications structure[cite: 1]. Conducted consensus workshops with regional CIOs and secured formal sign-off from the Architecture Board[cite: 1, 2].

Step 5 Action: Tailoring TOGAF 10 & Incorporating Series Guides

Customized the TOGAF ADM by incorporating the TOGAF Series Guide for Security Architecture and Digital Enterprise[cite: 1]. Defined draft and approved deliverable lifecycle states and extended the metamodel to support cloud service abstractions[cite: 1, 2].

Step 6 Action: Tooling Deployment & Request for Architecture Work

Deployed a SaaS-based EA repository tool configured with ArchiMate metamodels[cite: 1, 2]. Issued the official Request for Architecture Work to initiate Phase A for the "Global Cloud Core Modernization" initiative[cite: 1, 2].

4. Tailored Architecture Principles Catalog at Apex Financial

Sample of enduring principles defined in NSRI format during the Preliminary Phase at Apex[cite: 1].

BUSINESS PRINCIPLE
1. Cloud-First & API-Driven

Statement: All business capabilities must be exposed via standard APIs and deployed on elastic cloud infrastructure unless restricted by law[cite: 1].

Rationale: Enables rapid channel innovation, reduces legacy infrastructure maintenance, and allows global scaling[cite: 1].

Implications: On-premise deployments require explicit Architecture Board dispensation; legacy monoliths must be wrapped in API gateways[cite: 1].

DATA PRINCIPLE
2. Single Source of Truth for Customer Data

Statement: Customer master data must reside in a single globally governed master data service[cite: 1].

Rationale: Prevents data divergence across 14 country entities and eliminates GDPR compliance audit failures[cite: 1].

Implications: Regional applications are forbidden from storing local master copies of customer identity data[cite: 1].
