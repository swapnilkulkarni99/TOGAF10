Preliminary Phase: Architecture Capability

Core Value Proposition

Establishes the "Where, What, Why, Who, and How" of Enterprise Architecture capability.

💡
Must-Know Foundation Fact: The Preliminary Phase is the foundational preparation phase executed before initiating formal ADM cycles (Phases A through H). Its main goal is to create and enable the enterprise's Architecture Capability, customize the TOGAF framework, and establish governing Architecture Principles.

Primary Purpose

Prepare the enterprise for successful architecture work. This includes tailoring the TOGAF framework, defining the Organizational Model for Enterprise Architecture, establishing governance resources, and formulating enduring Architecture Principles.

Precedes all cyclic ADM phase iterations.

The 5 Core Architectural Questions (Approach)

1. Where

Define the enterprise scope & impacted organizational boundaries.

2. Why

Identify key drivers, business imperatives & strategic context.

3. What

Set requirements & desired architecture capability scope.

4. Who

Establish the EA Team, roles, & Organizational Model.

5. How

Formulate Architecture Principles, Governance framework, & tooling strategy.

Core Purpose & Fundamental Content Alignment

The Preliminary Phase creates an operational framework that allows Enterprise Architecture teams to operate seamlessly alongside other governance capabilities. By establishing clear reporting lines, C-level executive support, and standard tooling, the phase ensures that future architecture iterations deliver measurable business value.

Detailed Phase Objectives

Objective Focus Key Activities Target Deliverable / Outcome
Capability Assessment Review organizational context; determine desired EA maturity level. Capability Maturity Baseline & Target Assessment.
Framework Customization Tailor the TOGAF ADM, deliverables, tools, and terminology. Tailored Architecture Framework.
Governance & Organization Define EA team roles, reporting lines, and Architecture Board. Organizational Model for EA & Governance Framework.
Architecture Principles Establish enduring guidelines for Business, Data, App, & Tech. Architecture Principles Catalog.
Tooling & Repository Select modeling tools and set up the Architecture Repository. Configured Architecture Repository & Tools.
⚠️
Exam Pitfall: Do not confuse Architecture Principles (defined in the Preliminary Phase) with Business Principles (which are confirmed and elaborated in Phase A). Principles defined in the Preliminary Phase guide all downstream target architecture decisions across all ADM cycles!

Preliminary Phase Step-by-Step Activities

Select a step below to inspect detailed activity breakdowns, inputs, and high-yield study notes.

Step 1: Scope the Enterprise Organizations Impacted

Identify core business units, departments, strategic partners, and external ecosystems affected by the Architecture Capability. Enterprise scope can range from the entire corporation down to a single business unit or division.

  • Determine organizational breadth and authority boundaries.
  • Identify impacted business units and executive sponsors.
  • Assess existing strategic planning and budgeting cycles.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The scope of the architecture activity is bounded by financial resources, organizational authority, and stakeholder concerns.

Step 2: Confirm Governance and Support Frameworks

Review and align intersecting management frameworks operating in the enterprise, such as ITIL for service management, COBIT for IT governance, SAFe/Scrum for agile software development, and PRINCE2/PMBOK for project management.

  • Map EA touchpoints into project delivery and agile sprint backlogs.
  • Align architecture compliance checkpoints with corporate risk governance.
  • Establish touchpoints with business portfolio management.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: TOGAF is designed to complement and integrate seamlessly with non-TOGAF management frameworks.

Step 3: Define and Establish EA Team and Organization

Formalize the EA team structure, define architect roles and skill profiles, establish executive reporting lines, and form the Architecture Board to oversee architectural decisions.

  • Define roles and responsibilities for Enterprise Architects and Domain Architects.
  • Establish the Architecture Board charter, decision rights, and meeting cadences.
  • Assess skill gaps and establish training plans for the EA team.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The Organizational Model for EA formally defines team structure, decision rights, and governance roles.

Step 4: Identify and Establish Architecture Principles

Develop enduring general rules and guidelines across Business, Data, Application, and Technology domains that govern all future target architectures and implementation decisions.

  • Formulate principles using the standard 4-part structure: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications.
  • Validate principles against the 5 key criteria: Complete, Consistent, Stable, Understandable, Robust.
  • Secure formal endorsement from executive leadership and the Architecture Board.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: Architecture Principles are enduring rules intended to guide decisions and are seldom amended once ratified.

Step 5: Tailor the TOGAF Framework

Adapt TOGAF Fundamental Content, ADM phases, deliverables, and terminology to match the enterprise's unique culture, operating model, and technical domain requirements.

  • Integrate relevant TOGAF Series Guides (e.g., Security Architecture, Agile Integration, Digital Enterprise).
  • Customize deliverable templates and define enterprise metamodel extensions.
  • Document the Tailored Architecture Framework.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: Tailoring produces the approved deliverable known as the Tailored Architecture Framework.

Step 6: Develop Strategy & Implementation Plan for Tools

Select and deploy EA modeling software, configure the Architecture Repository classification structure, and generate the formal Request for Architecture Work to initiate ADM Phase A.

  • Configure repository partitions for Landscape, Standards Library, and Governance Repository.
  • Implement modeling tools aligned with ArchiMate or UML standards.
  • Issue the Request for Architecture Work to trigger the first formal ADM cycle.

🎯 Exam Takeaway: The primary triggering output sent from the Preliminary Phase to Phase A is the Request for Architecture Work.

Phase Inputs

Mandatory Strategic Inputs:
  • Organizational context, strategic plans, and business goals.
  • Sponsorship details, corporate mission statements, and budget constraints.
  • Descriptions of existing enterprise and IT systems.
Framework & Governance Inputs:
  • Existing management frameworks (ITIL, COBIT, PRINCE2, SAFe).
  • Enterprise Continuum & Standards Library assets.

Phase Outputs & Status Conventions

APPROVED
Tailored Architecture Framework: Customized ADM, methods, and deliverable structures.
APPROVED
Organizational Model for EA: Scoped team structure, roles, and review board charter.
APPROVED
Architecture Principles Catalog: Enduring rules across Business, Data, App, & Tech.
CREATED
Request for Architecture Work: Official document sent to trigger Phase A.

Industry Case Study: Global Bank Digital Transformation

Executing the Preliminary Phase for a Tier-1 Financial Institution

Problem

A retail bank operating across 10 regions suffered from duplicated IT software spend, conflicting regional architecture frameworks, lack of central oversight, and recurring GDPR compliance risks.

EA Application

Executed Preliminary Phase: Scoped global divisions, tailored TOGAF 10 with SAFe & ITIL, established an Architecture Board chaired by the CTO, and published 8 core Architecture Principles.

Results & Outputs

Issued approved Request for Architecture Work, configured a central Architecture Repository, reduced software duplication by 30%, and achieved 100% regulatory review pass rate.

Key Execution Highlights from the Case Study

Maturity Target Setup:

Defined a 2-year Capability Maturity roadmap from Level 1 (Ad Hoc) to Level 3 (Defined Capability).

Tooling Strategy:

Selected an ArchiMate-compliant modeling software integrated into ServiceNow for real-time application portfolio tracking.

🎯
High-Yield Exam Focus: The Preliminary Phase generates the Request for Architecture Work. It does NOT generate the Statement of Architecture Work or the Architecture Vision (both are produced in Phase A).

Key Mnemonics & High-Yield Rules

Mnemonic: NSRI (Architecture Principle Template) Every principle template MUST contain: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications.
Mnemonic: 5 Criteria for Good Principles (CCSUR) Complete, Consistent, Stable, Understandable, Robust.
Mnemonic: 5 Core Questions Where, Why, What, Who, How (Enterprise scope, Context, Requirements, Team, Principles/Governance).

TOGAF 10th Edition Key Terminology Callouts

Modular Structure: TOGAF 10 replaces monolithic manuals with two parts: TOGAF Fundamental Content (Core concepts & ADM) and TOGAF Series Guides (Practical guidance for Agile, Digital, Security).
Agile & Digital Alignment: Preliminary phase explicitly guides tailoring the ADM to work concurrently with Agile team iterations and digital transformation initiatives.
