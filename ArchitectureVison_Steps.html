Core Purpose of Phase A

Phase A sets the formal baseline and target vision for an enterprise architecture project[cite: 1]. It verifies that the project aligns with corporate strategic drivers, identifies executive stakeholders, defines clear scope parameters, evaluates organizational readiness for transformation, and constructs a business case to secure executive approval to proceed[cite: 1].

Primary Deliverable

Statement of Architecture Work: Acts as the formal contract between the architecture team and project sponsors[cite: 1].

Architectural Vision

High-level summary of Baseline & Target architectures across all 4 domains, supported by Solution Concept Diagrams[cite: 1].

High-Yield Exam Takeaway

Transparency vs. Selling: TOGAF 10 explicitly highlights that architects should not attempt to "sell" their architecture[cite: 1].

Instead, you develop a business case and value proposition to provide total transparency so stakeholders can make fully informed decisions[cite: 1].

Watch out for misleading options on the exam!

The 4 Scoping Dimensions (Step 6)

Scoping limits the architectural effort to ensure manageable deliverables within time and budget constraints[cite: 1].

1

Breadth

Full extent of the enterprise vs. specific business units, departments, or core processes targeted by this effort[cite: 1].

2

Depth

How detailed the architecture will be described[cite: 1]. Note: Baseline is often kept at a higher abstraction than Target[cite: 1].

3

Architecture Domains

Which of the 4 domains (Business, Data, Application, Technology - BDAT) are in scope for this iteration[cite: 1].

4

Time Period

The timeframe articulated for the Architecture Vision (e.g., 3-year strategic target vision vs. 1-year interim states)[cite: 1].

TOGAF 10 Capability Assessment Rules

1. Architectural Capabilities (EA Team) Evaluate if the EA team has the skills and bandwidth[cite: 1]. If gaps exist, you must cycle back to the Preliminary Phase to resolve them before continuing Phase A[cite: 1].
2. Business Capabilities (Enterprise) Identify high-level required business capabilities from corporate strategy[cite: 1]. Detailed capability gap analysis occurs in Phase B, not Phase A[cite: 1]!

TOGAF Standard 10th Edition Sequential Recommended Flow[cite: 1]

Actions: Align with corporate project management frameworks, secure project visibility, and confirm EA team composition, experience, and capability to deliver[cite: 1].

Exam Focus: Know PM framework alignment rules. Ensure official visibility before starting deep work[cite: 1].

Actions: Construct Stakeholder Map to plan engagement, map concerns to architecture viewpoints, build communications plan, and capture requirements[cite: 1].

Requirements Routing: In-scope requirements are stored in the Architecture Requirements Specification. Out-of-scope requirements are sent to the Requirements Repository for future iterations[cite: 1].

Actions: Clarify existing strategy or define strategic drivers with management[cite: 1]. Separate project-specific constraints (timeline, budget) from enterprise-wide constraints (e.g., mandatory COTS products derived from principles)[cite: 1].

Actions: Evaluate EA team capability and high-level enterprise business capabilities[cite: 1].

Exam Trap: EA capability gaps require cycling back to the Preliminary Phase[cite: 1]! Detailed business capability gap analysis belongs in Phase B[cite: 1].

Actions: Use the TOGAF Business Transformation Readiness Assessment (BTRA) technique to evaluate change readiness[cite: 1].

3 Main Purposes: (1) Shape architecture scope, (2) Identify project communication activities, (3) Identify change risk areas[cite: 1].

Actions: Establish boundary parameters across 4 dimensions: Breadth, Depth, Domains (BDAT), and Time Period[cite: 1]. Identify reusable assets from the Architecture Repository[cite: 1].

Actions: Review enterprise architecture and business principles established in the Preliminary Phase that govern this specific project scope[cite: 1].

Actions: Draft high-level Baseline & Target models across domains[cite: 1]. Use business scenarios to uncover requirements and create informal Solution Concept Diagrams[cite: 1].

Actions: Formulate tailored value propositions per stakeholder group, define KPIs/metrics, assess procurement needs, and build the business case to create full transparency[cite: 1].

Actions: Assess initial risk levels, potential impact/frequency, and assign mitigation strategies to reduce risk likelihood or severity[cite: 1].

Actions: Consolidate findings into the Statement of Architecture Work and obtain formal executive sponsor sign-off to proceed with full EA development[cite: 1].

Real-World Enterprise Scenario

Global Bank Core Modernization & Cloud Migration

Applying TOGAF 10 Phase A to align stakeholders, set boundaries, and secure executive approval.

The Challenge

"OmniBank" suffers from legacy mainframes, high maintenance costs, and slow feature delivery. Executive leadership wants to migrate to a modern cloud-native architecture, but stakeholders disagree on scope, budget, and implementation timelines.

Phase A Execution

  • • Scoping: Narrowed scope to retail banking services only for Year 1 (Breadth)[cite: 1].
  • • BTRA Assessment: Uncovered high risk in branch staff change readiness[cite: 1].
  • • Solution Concept: Drew high-level hybrid cloud solution diagram showing 30% latency reduction[cite: 1].

Phase A Outcome

Formulated value propositions per executive stakeholder group[cite: 1]. Produced the Statement of Architecture Work signed off by the CIO and Retail Banking VP, granting $2.5M budget to proceed to Phase B[cite: 1].

3 Top TOGAF 10 Exam Traps

  • 1. Trap: Thinking detailed business capability gap analysis happens in Phase A. Fact: It belongs in Phase B[cite: 1].
  • 2. Trap: Requiring equal depth for baseline and target architectures. Fact: Baseline is often kept higher-level[cite: 1].
  • 3. Trap: Expecting Phase A out-of-scope requirements to be discarded. Fact: Stored in Requirements Repository[cite: 1].
